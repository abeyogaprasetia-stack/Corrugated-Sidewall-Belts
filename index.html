Questions

A rubber conveyor belt with flexible, wave-shaped walls vulcanized along both edges and cleats across the belt between them. The walls and cleats form pockets that hold bulk material at inclines from 30° up to 90°, where a flat belt is limited to about 18–22°. The base belt carries all the tension; the sidewalls and cleats only contain the material.

The difference is in the base belt. A flexible base belt bends across its width and is the standard construction for sidewall belts. A cross-rigid base belt adds stiff transverse plies so the belt stays flat between the sidewalls with support only at the edges, which matters on wide belts and long unsupported spans. Belt construction: cross-rigid vs flexible.
